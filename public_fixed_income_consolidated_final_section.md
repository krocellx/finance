# Public Fixed Income — AI concentration

## Conclusion

AI concentration in public fixed income is already material, but there is no defensible single percentage for the whole asset class. The most decision-useful current measures are **6.2% of the US high-grade corporate market for a narrow, direct AI-buildout cohort** and **2.75–3.0% of the US high-yield index for high-performance-computing (HPC) issuers**. A broader high-grade ecosystem measure reaches **15.5%**, but it includes utilities and industrial suppliers whose debt is only partly exposed to AI and should therefore be treated as a common-factor risk envelope, not as 15.5% of bonds whose proceeds are wholly AI-related. The five largest hyperscalers alone are **4.15% of the J.P. Morgan US Liquid Index (JULI)**.

The clearest warning comes from issuance rather than the existing stock. In 2026 through mid-August, tracked global corporate and syndicated AI-linked financing was approximately **$417bn**, versus **$189bn in all of 2025**; this includes non-US, loan and private-placement channels that are not all eligible for a public bond index. HPC issuers represented **$31.1bn, or 40.4%, of non-refinancing US high-yield issuance** through mid-June despite being less than 3% of the index. These figures are not contradictory: they use different universes and show how quickly a large financing flow is beginning to change a much larger outstanding debt stock. [Morgan Stanley, *A Break from the Heat—For Now*, pp. 1–6](<research/Morgan_Stanley-US_Credit_Strategy_A_Break_from_the_Heat_For_Now-2026-08-14.pdf>); [J.P. Morgan, *AI Capex 2.0*, pp. 32–41](<research/JP_Morgan-AI_Capex_2_0_If_You_Build_It_They_Will_Finance_It_Updated_Forecasts-2026-06-17.pdf>).

On a transparent scenario basis, the narrow high-grade buildout sleeve could reach **about 8–12% by end-2030** (base case **10.0%**), while the broad ecosystem could exceed **20%**. HPC high yield could reach **roughly 6–11% by end-2030** (base case **8.5%**), although the range is lower confidence because bonds can refinance, amortise, migrate to investment grade, or move into loans and securitizations. These are planning ranges, not point forecasts.

## 1. What concentration means and where it stands today

For credit, AI exposure should mean **the market value of debt whose repayment, spread or refinancing risk is materially linked to the AI investment cycle**. It has three distinct channels:

1. **Direct buildout:** hyperscalers, data-centre developers, semiconductor firms and HPC/neocloud borrowers raising debt to fund compute capacity.
2. **Enabling ecosystem:** utilities, power and electrical equipment, cooling, networking and other suppliers whose credit profile is increasingly tied to AI-related orders or contracted load.
3. **Disruption exposure:** incumbent software and services borrowers whose cash flows may be weakened by AI. This is a negative exposure and must not be added to buildout financing as though it were the same risk.

Concentration should be reported separately by rating and legal wrapper. An unsecured hyperscaler bond, an HPC secured bond, a data-centre ABS tranche and an SPV construction bond can share the same tenant or capex driver, but have different default, duration and collateral risks.

The apparent current-number conflict is therefore mostly definitional:

- **4.15%** is five named issuers; **6.2%** adds direct data-centre and semiconductor borrowers; **15.5%** adds the enabling ecosystem. These are nested measures, not estimates to average or add.
- The Bank of England's **3% at end-2025** for the same five hyperscalers is USD investment-grade debt on an earlier date and a different benchmark. Its finding that they exceeded **15% of early-2026 issuance** is a flow measure. [Bank of England, *Financial Stability Report*, July 2026, pp. 26–28](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/-/media/boe/files/financial-stability-report/2026/financial-stability-report-july-2026.pdf).
- J.P. Morgan's **$165bn** of 2026 high-grade issuance covers the narrow buildout cohort through mid-June. Morgan Stanley's later **$417bn** covers a broader global set of US and non-US IG, HY, loans and chip-related financing through mid-August. The latter's rounded components sum to about $415bn; the $2bn difference is rounding.
- The **40.4% HY flow share** is `$31.1bn / $76.9bn` of non-refinancing issuance, not 40% of the outstanding HY market. It should be viewed as a leading indicator of future stock concentration.

## 2. Recommended measurement framework

The most defensible approach is a **stock–flow–look-through framework**, maintained separately for investment grade, high yield, loans and securitized credit.

### Stock

For each benchmark (B), measure the full market value of identified bonds rather than applying a generic revenue haircut:

\[
C^{stock}_{B,t}=\frac{\sum_i MV_{i,t}\times A_{i,t}}{\sum_{j\in B}MV_{j,t}},
\]

where (A=1) for directly classified buildout securities and (A\in[0,1]) only where an enabling issuer's AI-attributable exposure can be supported by a contract, customer, load, capacity or use-of-proceeds measure. A bond is a claim on the whole obligor; an arbitrary 25% or 50% haircut based on AI revenue would therefore misstate direct credit exposure. Where no defensible attribution exists, report the issuer in the broad envelope and mark the exposure unknown rather than assigning a precise score.

### Flow

Measure new borrowing over an identical currency, rating, instrument and time universe:

\[
C^{flow}_{B,t}=\frac{\text{AI-linked gross or net issuance in }B}{\text{total gross or net issuance in }B}.
\]

Gross and non-refinancing flow must not be mixed. Report the **flow–stock gap**, (C^{flow}-C^{stock}), as a pressure indicator; do not call the flow share the current portfolio weight.

### Look-through and risk

Map issuer, parent, SPV, guarantor, tenant/offtaker, collateral, currency, rating and maturity. Report both legal-obligor and ultimate-economic-counterparty concentration, because at least **$53bn of investment-grade data-centre construction bonds** was outside standard indices and hyperscaler leases/purchase commitments can generate third-party debt. This mapping should also prevent double counting when construction loans refinance into ABS/CMBS or when HY issuers migrate to IG. [Morgan Stanley, *Data Center/AI Financing: Investor Feedback*, pp. 3–16](<research/Morgan_Stanley-US_Corporate_and_Securitized_Credit_Data_Center_AI_Financing_Investor_Feedback-2026-07-13.pdf>).

Market-value concentration should be supplemented by spread duration, expected loss, secured/unsecured status, maturity walls and top-tenant share. The result is both an exposure weight and a measure of sensitivity to a common AI-capex shock; the two are not interchangeable.

## 3. Forward concentration

### Investment grade

J.P. Morgan forecasts **$2.1tn of high-grade AI-buildout borrowing over five years**, including 2026 year-to-date issuance, as part of an estimated **$4.1tn of debt financing for $5.5tn of AI capex through 2030**. Its 2026 bottom-up bridge is $150bn of USD hyperscaler debt, $100bn non-USD and $170bn of data-centre/semiconductor structures, plus $5–15bn of municipal prepayment bonds. This is gross financing across currencies and structures, not automatic growth in a US high-grade index. [J.P. Morgan, *AI Capex 2.0*, pp. 1–3 and 32–35](<research/JP_Morgan-AI_Capex_2_0_If_You_Build_It_They_Will_Finance_It_Updated_Forecasts-2026-06-17.pdf>).

The narrow-stock scenario starts with $560bn and a $9.03tn implied market. It subtracts the $165bn already issued in 2026 from the $2.1tn five-year gross forecast, then applies an **index-eligible net-retention factor** to the remaining $1.935tn. That factor captures non-USD and off-index issuance, refinancing, maturities and structures that do not remain in the chosen high-grade benchmark. Retained AI debt is added to both the numerator and denominator; the exponent is the approximately 4.5 years from mid-2026 to end-2030.

| End-2030 narrow IG scenario | Net retention of remaining gross issuance* | Rest-of-index growth | Calculation | Result |
|---|---:|---:|---|---:|
| Low | 20% | 5% p.a. | `(0.560 + 1.935 × 20%) / (9.03 × 1.05^4.5 + 1.935 × 20%)` | **8.1%** |
| Base | 30% | 4% p.a. | `(0.560 + 1.935 × 30%) / (9.03 × 1.04^4.5 + 1.935 × 30%)` | **10.0%** |
| High | 40% | 3% p.a. | `(0.560 + 1.935 × 40%) / (9.03 × 1.03^4.5 + 1.935 × 40%)` | **12.0%** |

\*The 20–40% factor is an explicit judgement assumption, not a sell-side forecast. It should be replaced with a deal-level eligibility, maturity and refinancing schedule when available.

The factor translates into $387–774bn of net additional index debt. As a reasonableness check, Morgan Stanley estimates that a roughly $10tn US IG market could absorb **$679–711bn** before the relevant exposure reached prior 14–15% sector-weight peaks; a separate largest-issuer test gives approximately $796bn for the five hyperscalers. Those are historical market-capacity reference points, not forecasts, but they support the order of magnitude of the scenario's upper half. [Morgan Stanley, *A Break from the Heat—For Now*, pp. 7–11](<research/Morgan_Stanley-US_Credit_Strategy_A_Break_from_the_Heat_For_Now-2026-08-14.pdf>).

Two published cross-checks bracket this range. Apollo's narrower five-hyperscaler scenario rises from **3.0% at end-2025 to 6.8% by 2030**, assuming the five firms issue IG debt equal to 20% of consensus capex and the rest of the index grows 5% annually. The five names had already reached 4.15% in JULI by June 2026, so **6–8%** remains a reasonable 2030 core-obligor range, although Apollo's inputs now need refreshing. [Apollo, *2026 Credit Outlook*, pp. 6–7](https://www.apollo.com/content/dam/apolloaem/documents/insights/outlook/apollo-global-2026-credit-outlook.pdf). At the other end, J.P. Morgan's earlier 75-issuer ecosystem work projected a move from **14.5% to more than 20% by 2030**; the June 2026 update raised the current broad measure to 15.5% and its five-year borrowing forecast from $1.5tn to $2.1tn. **More than 20% is therefore a plausible broad-factor envelope, not a security-level forecast.** [Bank of England, *Financial Stability Report*, December 2025](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/financial-stability-report/2025/december-2025); [J.P. Morgan, *AI Capex 2.0*, pp. 32–35](<research/JP_Morgan-AI_Capex_2_0_If_You_Build_It_They_Will_Finance_It_Updated_Forecasts-2026-06-17.pdf>).

### High yield and structured credit

J.P. Morgan expects **$80bn of HPC leveraged-finance issuance in 2026** ($65bn bonds and $15bn loans), taking HPC to approximately **4.5% of the HY index by year-end** and about 1% of the loan index. It forecasts **$350bn of cumulative HPC bonds and loans in 2026–30**, peaking in 2027. [J.P. Morgan, *AI Capex 2.0*, pp. 36–41](<research/JP_Morgan-AI_Capex_2_0_If_You_Build_It_They_Will_Finance_It_Updated_Forecasts-2026-06-17.pdf>).

For 2030, start with $39.8bn of HPC HY debt and the $1.45tn implied index. From the $350bn cumulative forecast, remove the $31.1bn of known 2026 HY issuance and $3.1bn first HPC loan, assume **60–80%** of the remaining $315.8bn is issued as HY bonds, retain **40–60%** in the HY index after refinancing, amortisation and migration, and grow the baseline index by **2–4% annually**. Retained HPC debt is again added to both numerator and denominator. The low case is `(39.8 + 315.8 × 60% × 40%) / (1,447 × 1.04^4.5 + 315.8 × 60% × 40%) = 6.4%`; the high case is **11.0%** using 80%, 60% and 2%, respectively. The midpoint is **8.5%** (70% bond share, 50% retention and 3% index growth). Existing HPC debt is assumed to refinance back into the index; failure to do so would lower the result. This range is deliberately wide: average HPC bond duration is below 3.5 years, and secured/project debt can refinance into ABS, CMBS or IG. The scenario adds $76–152bn of net HPC HY debt, below Morgan Stanley's approximate **$170bn** capacity estimate to a 15% historical sector-weight threshold; that threshold is a market-capacity comparison, not a safe credit limit or forecast. [Morgan Stanley, *A Break from the Heat—For Now*, pp. 7–11](<research/Morgan_Stanley-US_Credit_Strategy_A_Break_from_the_Heat_For_Now-2026-08-14.pdf>).

Structured credit is likely to absorb part of that migration. The Structured Finance Association projects data-centre securitizations rising from approximately **$61bn in 2026 to $180bn by end-2028**, reaching about **20% of esoteric ABS and 15% of SASB CMBS**. That is a concentration forecast within two structured-credit subsectors, not within the whole bond market. [Structured Finance Association, July 2026](https://structuredfinance.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/SFA-Research-Corner_How-Data-Center-ABS-and-CMBS-Fit-in-a-Broader-Financing-Ecosystem.pdf).

## 4. Evidence, assumptions and remaining gaps

The directional conclusion is strong: AI financing is broadening from a small group of cash-rich hyperscalers into data-centre, semiconductor, power and structured-credit borrowers, and current issuance is running well ahead of stock weights. The precise future weights are less certain.

- **Constituent transparency:** the five-name hyperscaler basket is reproducible, but J.P. Morgan does not publish the full narrow, broad or 12-name HPC security lists. The 6.2%, 15.5% and 2.75% figures are reported observations, while their implied market sizes are calculations. The source-linked audit bridge is in [the reconciliation workbook](<ai_reponse/ai-credit-stock-flow-rebuild.xlsx>).
- **Eligibility and denominator:** the $2.1tn and $350bn forecasts span currencies and legal wrappers. Index inclusion, issue size, rating, refinancing and maturities are not supplied deal by deal. This is why the scenario uses explicit retention assumptions rather than adding gross issuance to current index stock.
- **Broad-ecosystem attribution:** 15.5% treats whole utility and industrial issuers as part of the ecosystem. Without contract-level load, customer or backlog shares, it is an upper exposure envelope. The narrow 6.2% measure should remain the headline stock estimate.
- **Hidden and shifting exposure:** SPVs, private placements, municipal prepays, leases and purchase commitments can be off-index, while later securitization can make one project appear in several issuance datasets. The five hyperscalers disclose more than $660bn of leases and more than $640bn of purchase commitments, but these categories overlap and have different timing; they cannot simply be added to debt. Missing exposure is unknown, not zero, and cumulative issuance across wrappers must be deduplicated. [Morgan Stanley, *Global Fixed Income Outlook*, pp. 12–18](<research/Morgan_Stanley-Global_Fixed_Income_Outlook_April_2026-2026-04-13.pdf>).
- **Forecast scope has changed:** Morgan Stanley's older $2.9tn data-centre-spending estimate covered 2025–28 and excluded power, while J.P. Morgan's $5.5tn runs through 2030 and captures a broader, later buildout. The difference is mostly horizon and scope, not a like-for-like revision. Morgan Stanley itself described the earlier $1.5tn financing-gap allocation as assumption-heavy. [Morgan Stanley, *Bridging a $1.5tn Data Center Financing Gap*, pp. 1–16](<research/Morgan_Stanley-Global_Fixed_Income_Tech_Diffusion_and_GenAI_Bridging_a_1_5tr_Data_Center_Financing_Gap-2025-07-16.pdf>).
- **Credit risk is not market-value weight:** hyperscaler IG debt may have low default risk even as its index weight rises; highly levered HPC and software borrowers have much larger refinancing and loss sensitivity. Concentration reporting should therefore pair weights with spread duration, rating, maturity, collateral and tenant/offtaker concentration.
- **Disruption is a separate tail:** sector weights of 3.5% in HY and 16% in loans are useful screening envelopes, but they neither establish that every software borrower is impaired nor measure loss severity. Morgan Stanley notes that more than 80% of software loan issuers are private/sponsor-backed and that definitions vary across data providers. [Morgan Stanley, *Global Fixed Income Outlook*, pp. 12–22](<research/Morgan_Stanley-Global_Fixed_Income_Outlook_April_2026-2026-04-13.pdf>); [Morgan Stanley, *Mapping Software Exposure in Leveraged Credit*, pp. 1–15](<research/Morgan_Stanley-Global_Credit_and_CLO_Strategy_Mapping_Software_Exposure_in_Leveraged_Credit-2026-02-09.pdf>).

The practical conclusion is to monitor **6.2% narrow IG and 2.75–3.0% HPC HY as current stock**, **15.5% broad IG as the common-factor envelope**, and stock/flow gaps and wrapper migration as the leading indicators. The end-2030 planning ranges are **8–12% narrow IG**, **more than 20% broad IG**, and **6–11% HPC HY**, subject to the explicit eligibility, retention and market-growth assumptions above.
